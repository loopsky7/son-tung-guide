💬Q&A

1. Is he really the most famous Vietnamese pop star?

Mostly yes on YouTube, records and press, but not on Spotify.

  • YouTube: His channel has 11.9M subscribers and 3.74B total views (read from the official channel, 21 Oct 2026). On Soundcharts' list of Vietnamese artists by YouTube subscribers he's #1 with 12M. Next are Hậu Hoàng (8.4M), Jack (6.5M), Misthy (6.4M) and Đen (5.2M) (accessed 21 Oct 2026).
  • First Vietnamese artist with a YouTube Diamond Play Button (10M subscribers), reached on 5 July 2022. He was also the first Vietnamese artist past 1B views (VnExpress Intl, 6 Jul 2022).
  • Spotify: He has 1.9M monthly listeners (Spotify, 21 Oct 2026). That puts him only #8 among Vietnamese artists, behind HDN (3.2M), tienanh109 (3.1M), Hwungii, Dangrangto, JustaTee, GREY D and DJ VGK1 (Soundcharts, accessed 21 Oct 2026). VnExpress put his lifetime Spotify streams at more than 810M (30 May 2026).
  • Billboard: "Muộn Rồi Mà Sao Còn" reached #126 on the Billboard Global Excl. US chart (chart, week of 15 May 2021). Tuổi Trẻ called him the first Vietnamese artist on that chart (Tuổi Trẻ). "Đừng Làm Trái Tim Anh Đau" reached #162 (chart, 22 Jun 2024). "Making My Way" hit #1 on the Billboard Vietnam Hot 100 in May 2023 (Ngôi Sao, quoting Billboard VN, 18 May 2023).
  • Awards: He won Best Southeast Asia Act at the 2015 MTV EMAs and was on Forbes Vietnam's 30 Under 30 in 2018 (VnExpress Intl).
  • Verdict for Quang: He's fair to call him the biggest single name, judging by YouTube and media records. On Spotify, though, he's one of a pack, with a newer rap and indie generation ahead of him. I found no social-follower figure I could source and date, so I left that out.

3. "Come My Way" with Tyga: is it real, and is it amapiano?

  • It's real. Official MV, released 28 May 2026 (Apple Music, Spotify).
  • Label: The release line reads "℗ 2026 M-TP Entertainment Ltd, exclusively licensed to MMusic Records" (Apple and Spotify). Korea's Bugs adds Virgin Music Group, with Universal Music Korea as the distributor (Bugs). I couldn't confirm who owns MMusic Records or where it's based.
  • Credits (Muso.ai): producer, mixing and mastering by Marvey Muzique. Writers are Tyga and Marvey Muzique (lyrics and composition) and Sơn Tùng (composition). Tiền Phong describes Marvey Muzique as a Nigerian beatmaker who specialises in Afrobeat (Tiền Phong).
  • Genre: mostly Afrobeats, not amapiano.
    • Apple lists it as "Afro-Beat".
    • VnExpress says it "draws influence from Afrobeats" and uses auto-tune in places.
    • Tiền Phong calls it an Afrobeat track.
    • Only LiFTED Asia calls it "Amapiano-inspired".
    • No source I found mentions log drums or shakers.
    • Tempo: Snaplyrics lists 145 BPM, D minor (7A). That's an algorithmic estimate and may be double-time. I found no second database to confirm it, so tap it yourself.
  • What the lyrics are about: VnExpress sums it up as a man whose life is changed by a woman, quoting "Call me your prisoner / I'm jailed in your love I ain't coming out." Sơn Tùng told Hypebeast that the collab with Tyga, who has Vietnamese heritage, was about both of them "reconnect[ing] with our roots" (Hypebeast, 1 Jun 2026).

4. Who made the MV, and was AI used?

  • Director: Phương Vũ. Production house is Antiantiart, art department Microwave Soups, and the LA unit was More Avenue (Antiantiart's official credits).
  • VFX/CGI credited:
    • Dragon Street Studio (wall-of-death transition, painting scenes)
    • Tóth-Horgosi Gergely "Tehage" (3D, compositing)
    • Infinite VFX (bull-riding scene)
    • OMEDIA Studio (bullet time)
    • LumiGrade / 4DScanLab ("4DGS capture", plus Gaussian-splat cleanup)

      These are 3D capture and photogrammetry techniques, not generative AI. Tri Thức explains the bull-race shot as a multi-camera bullet-time rig finished with CGI (via Báo Mới).

  • AI: No source confirms AI was used, and I found no published press speculation about it either.
  • The real controversies:
    • Visual artist Lê Giang said the after-credits set resembled her 2017 installation Tàn chỉ. Antiantiart took responsibility, and on 9 Jun 2026 M-TP cut the scene. They said this was "not an admission of any legal conclusion" (Dân trí, Tuổi Trẻ).
    • Critics also objected to him standing on a Lạc bird statue (Vietnam.vn/Znews).

5. Where was it filmed? (You're right there!)

  • The official location credits name Tràng An, Bái Đính, Tam Cốc and Chùa Rô (An Giang). The four main shoot locations were LA downtown, Ninh Bình, Saigon and the Mekong Delta (Antiantiart).
  • VnExpress confirms Tràng An (UNESCO) and downtown LA. It also names the Bảy Núi bull-race festival in An Giang as a visual motif (VnExpress Intl).
  • Unconfirmed: which exact shots are Tam Cốc versus Tràng An or Bái Đính. No source mentions Hạ Long.

🚀Career

2. How did he get famous? (short version)

Born Nguyễn Thanh Tùng in Thái Bình. Around 2011 he was posting songs underground on the LadyKillah forum. "M-TP" started as a nickname there, "Mr. Tùng Pình" (2sao). His self-written "Cơn Mưa Ngang Qua" went viral and won Song of the Month on Bài hát yêu thích in Oct 2012. On 1 Nov 2012 he signed a 5-year deal with Huy Tuấn's Văn Production. "Em Của Ngày Hôm Qua" (late 2013) hit 100M audio plays in 3 months (VnExpress, 2015). After a contract fight (Văn Production sued him in Jan 2015) he was managed by Quang Huy's WePro. He left WePro in Dec 2016 (Tuổi Trẻ) and set up M-TP Entertainment in Nov 2016, with himself as chairman (VietnamBiz). "Lạc Trôi" was its first release (VnExpress, 12 Dec 2016).

Other records

  • Hãy Trao Cho Anh: 635K people watched the premiere live. It was the fastest Vietnamese MV to 10M views (3 hours) (VnExpress Intl, Jul 2019). Billboard also covered it (Billboard).
  • Chạy Ngay Đi: 22M views in 24 hours, reported as Asia's most-viewed MV in a day at the time (same VnExpress article). Wikipedia gives 17.6M, so the sources disagree.
  • Come My Way: #2 on YouTube's global 24-hour chart, per Kworb, cited by VnExpress.

Recurring collaborators

  • Triple D (Dương Đại Dương): from the Hanoi underground and the SpaceSpeakers crew, with a background in trap, house and dubstep. He produced Chúng Ta Không Thuộc Về Nhau and Lạc Trôi, and calls Sơn Tùng's talent "năng khiếu trời cho" (a God-given gift) (Thanh Niên).
  • Onionn:
    • Produced Chạy Ngay Đi, Hãy Trao Cho Anh, Có Chắc Yêu Là Đây and Chúng Ta Của Hiện Tại.
    • Signed exclusively to M-TP Entertainment in July 2020 and announced his exit on 5 Jan 2022.
    • Kenh14 notes that two of his productions for Sơn Tùng drew plagiarism suspicions.

      Source: Kenh14.

  • Khắc Hưng: credited only as arranger on Nơi Này Có Anh. I found no other Sơn Tùng credit for him.
  • Long Halo: mixed audio on Âm Thầm Bên Em and mastered Nơi Này Có Anh (YT credits).
  • Marvey Muzique:
    • Tiền Phong describes him as a Nigerian, Grammy-nominated Afrobeat producer. He was sound engineer on There's No One At All, then main producer on Come My Way (Tiền Phong).
    • Co-producer on Chúng Ta Của Tương Lai and Đừng Làm Trái Tim Anh Đau (Qobuz).
    • "Grammy-nominated" rests on Tiền Phong alone [Unverified].
  • M-TP Talent Team: his label's in-house production credit (Qobuz, 2024 singles). No individual members are named publicly.
  • SlimV: producer or remixer on some tracks of the 2017 compilation m-tp M-TP (Qobuz).

👔Styling

  • Long-time stylist: Phạm Bảo Luận. He had worked with Sơn Tùng for more than 4 years as of 2020. He says Tùng "usually buys his own outfits", for example several limited Louis Vuitton pieces in Có Chắc Yêu Là Đây (Harper's Bazaar VN, 24 Aug 2020).
  • Come My Way wardrobe: Quách Đắc Thắng ("Vicky Virus"), founder of La Lune.
    • Close to 10 looks, mostly custom-made, mixing cổ phục (traditional dress), áo dài, Đông Sơn Lạc-bird embroidery and a 3D-printed nón quai thao (traditional flat palm-leaf hat) (Hoa Học Trò / Tiền Phong).
    • In the Ninh Bình scene he wears a beige silk áo dài with distressed trousers, Nike sneakers, Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels bracelets and a Gucci fan.
    • Elsewhere: a fur jacket with jeans next to Tyga beside a DeLorean, and a tweed hoodie with leather Bermudas (VnExpress).
  • Gucci: named Gucci's first male "Friend of the House" in Vietnam in May 2023 (Media OutReach release). He sat front row at the Gucci Cruise 2024 show in Seoul (Harper's Bazaar VN).
  • Award: ELLE Style Awards 2017, Most Stylish Male Singer (ELLE VN).
  • Own fashion line:
    • He announced a brand called PlusEightyFour (+84), named after Vietnam's dialling code, in Dec 2019 (CafeBiz). I found no evidence it ever launched.
    • Confirmed merch: exclusive "There's No One At All" T-shirts sold via VinID, linked in the official MV description.
  • Signature look (as sourced): he buys his own luxury pieces (Bazaar) and mixes traditional Vietnamese dress with streetwear (VnExpress). CafeBiz compared the +84 concept to G-Dragon. Wider "fashion icon" claims appear only on Wikipedia [Unverified].

  • Name origin: a fan named Trang offered two options in 2012, "M-TP Fs" or "Sky". He picked Sky ("the sky protects and supports him"). It caught on after he greeted fans as Sky in a Christmas clip, and he later named the 2019 Sky Tour after them (Znews, 21 Nov 2019).
  • Fan club structure: I found no official membership or chapter figures [Not found].

2014: borrowed beats on early hits. Admitted + chart removal

  • What was similar: netizens matched the beats of Cơn Mưa Ngang Qua (to a 2010 Korean song by Namolla Family), Em Của Ngày Hôm Qua (to EXID's "Every Night"), Em Đừng Đi and others (Thanh Niên).
  • His words (4 Jun 2014, via his manager): "phần giai điệu là do tôi sáng tác, phần beat nhạc do tôi lấy nguồn trên mạng… Việc tôi sử dụng và lấy cảm hứng từ beat nhạc ngoại thì tôi thừa nhận". In English: "the melody is mine; the beat I took from the internet… I admit using and taking inspiration from foreign beats". He called it "cách làm quen thuộc và xu hướng sáng tác hiện nay của nhiều nghệ sĩ trẻ", "a familiar practice and a current songwriting trend among many young artists" (VietNamNet).
    • On Em Của Ngày Hôm Qua he said he used an online beat and had someone remake it for better sound quality.
    • He said his team had since steered him away from using foreign beats.
  • Outcome:
    • The Bài hát yêu thích chart removed Cơn Mưa Ngang Qua, Đừng Về Trễ and Em Của Ngày Hôm Qua from 14 Jun 2014. 11 of the 13 jury members who voted agreed.
    • The organisers said he had not broken the contest rules, but had breached professional ethics by using beats "không xin phép, không rõ nguồn gốc" (without permission, of unknown origin). His earlier awards were not revoked (Thanh Niên, VOV).
    • VOV says his label's music director, Huy Tuấn, asked for the songs to be withdrawn.

Late 2014: Chắc Ai Đó Sẽ Về vs "Because I Miss You" (Jung Yong-hwa). Ruled (administrative)

  • What was similar: the Department of Performing Arts found both songs use a slow-rock 6/8 rhythm at the same tempo in C major. It found the melodies "hoàn toàn khác nhau" (completely different) and the lyrics unrelated (Thanh Niên).
  • A separate panel disagreed: set up through the Vietnam Musicians' Association and the music copyright centre VCPMC, it had found the songs 60–70% similar (VOV).
  • Korean side: an email from FNC Entertainment, Jung Yong-hwa's agency, said that despite similarities it did not regard this as "ăn cắp bản quyền" (copyright theft) (Báo Xây dựng). The ministry panel later said the email had no legal weight because it did not come from the author.
  • His position: he said the melody and lyrics were his. The ministry's spokesman said he "thừa nhận phần beat (bản phối) là không phải của anh ta" (admitted the beat, i.e. the arrangement, was not his) (Báo Xây dựng).
  • Outcome, 5 Dec 2014: the culture ministry's panel ruled that the beat/harmony was "bị ảnh hưởng rất lớn" (very heavily influenced) by Because I Miss You. He had to replace it before the song could circulate. It was treated as a first violation, so he got a reminder, with fines threatened for a repeat (VnExpress).

2016: Chúng Ta Không Thuộc Về Nhau vs "We Don't Talk Anymore". Accusation only

  • Producer Triple D said he made the beat himself: "Các bài nhạc trùng vòng hoà âm là điều rất bình thường" ("songs sharing a chord loop is very normal"). He added that the songs share only the loop and the Tropical House genre, and "chả đoạn nào giống đoạn nào, kể cả về sound tiếng" ("no section is alike, not even the sounds") (Saostar, 4 Aug 2016).
  • No ruling was found.

Feb 2021: Chúng Ta Của Hiện Tại vs GC's "R&B All Night". Settled

  • What happened: UK beat-maker GC (Gary) filed a YouTube copyright claim and the MV was blocked on 22 Feb 2021 (Thanh Niên).
  • GC's account: the producer "đã thừa nhận rằng anh ta sao chép sản phẩm của tôi" (admitted copying my work) (Thanh Niên). Elsewhere GC said the team admitted using his beat "để tham khảo" (as a reference), but the arrangement, harmony and hook were too similar to call it reference (Dân trí).
  • Settlement: after talking to M-TP's CEO, GC wrote "we reached agreement… I've reinstated the video". The MV returned with its view count intact (Dân trí).
  • M-TP Entertainment's only public words: "Âm nhạc không biên giới. Một người bạn mới của M-TP Entertainment ở nơi phương xa" ("Music has no borders. A new friend of M-TP Entertainment from far away") (SGGP).
  • Talk of a US$100K payment is [Rumour]: Dân trí says "có thông tin cho rằng" ("there is information that").

Feb 2021: Có Chắc Yêu Là Đây vs Robin Wesley's "Lucky". Accusation, no public resolution

  • What happened: Dutch producer Robin Wesley re-posted his beat "Lucky" (dated 16 Nov 2019, "only usable with permission") titled as the Có Chắc Yêu Là Đây original instrumental (Thanh Niên).
  • His statement: "Rõ ràng là những sai lầm đã diễn ra… chúng tôi đang cố gắng giải quyết vấn đề này với nghệ sĩ" ("Clearly mistakes have been made… we are trying to resolve this with the artist") (Thanh Niên; SGGP).
  • M-TP's response: a representative declined to comment (VTC/Zing).
  • Outcome: none reported.
  • Both 2021 tracks were produced by Onionn. Kenh14 notes two of his Sơn Tùng productions drew plagiarism suspicion (Kenh14). I found no statement from Onionn himself.

May 2023: Making My Way vs "Unforgettable" (French Montana ft. Swae Lee). Accusation only

  • The claim: fans said the drums and intro were alike (Dân trí). Dân trí asked his team and got no reply.
  • Tiền Phong's analysis: the melody and chord loop are not the same. The shared element is the reggaeton/dancehall drum pattern at about 100 BPM, which is "yếu tố cơ bản của mỗi dòng nhạc" (a basic element of each genre). There was "chưa đủ cơ sở" (not enough basis) to call it plagiarism (Tiền Phong).

Jun 2026: Come My Way set vs Lê Giang's installation "Tàn Chỉ / Vestige of the Land" (2017). Admitted by the set designers + MV edited; legal outcome open

  • What was similar: the after-credits ruin set, with calcified reliefs and broken northern communal-house (đình làng) architecture around the column bases. The arts group Hanoi Grapevine flagged it on 2 Jun (VCCA).
  • Microwave Soups (art direction and set design): admitted it used her work as a reference without permission or credit and apologised (Thanh Niên).
  • Antiantiart (production company): took responsibility and said M-TP Talent was only the client (VCCA).
  • Lê Giang: did not accept the apology (Tuổi Trẻ Cười).
  • M-TP Talent, 9 Jun: cut the scene and said the edit "không phản ánh hay thừa nhận bất kỳ kết luận pháp lý nào" ("does not reflect or admit any legal conclusion") (VietNamNet, Dân trí).
  • Lawyer's view: removing the scene does not erase liability if an infringement happened (Tuổi Trẻ Cười).

"Technique and coincidence as inspiration"? What the record actually says

  • "Inspiration" is his own word, but only from 2014. He admitted using foreign beats and called it "lấy cảm hứng" (taking inspiration). The authorities treated that as using someone else's beat, not as inspiration.
  • No source quotes Sơn Tùng or M-TP using "coincidence" (trùng hợp). The nearest is Triple D's 2016 point that shared chord loops in the same genre are normal.
  • In 2021 the "reference" (tham khảo) framing came from GC's account of the team. GC rejected it as an excuse.
  • Since 2021 the pattern is silence or settlement. Dân trí notes he "luôn chọn cách im lặng" (always chooses to stay silent) (Dân trí 2023).

🧠How to borrow without copying

3. How to do it legitimately: inspiration vs copying (DJ/producer learner)

General information, mostly US law plus industry practice. Not legal advice. Laws differ by country.

Generally free to use (ideas and building blocks)

  • Ideas, methods and systems are not protected; only their specific expression is (US Copyright Office, Circular 33). Production techniques fall here: sidechain, the way a drop is built, a vocal-chop method.
  • "Common property" musical material is not original authorship: scales, arpeggios, "chord symbols based on standard chord progressions" (Compendium III §802.5(A)).
  • Court rulings:
    • Descending chromatic scales, arpeggios and short note sequences are "common musical elements" (Skidmore v. Led Zeppelin, 9th Cir. 2020).
    • "Commonplace musical elements… building blocks belong in the public domain". Katy Perry's "Dark Horse" was found not to infringe (Gray v. Hudson, 9th Cir. 2022).
  • Genre drum patterns and tempo: the Making My Way analysis above is the practical example (Tiền Phong). Copying a specific programmed beat or arrangement is a different matter (see below).

Risky

  • Melody/topline and distinctive hooks. These are the core of a musical work: melody, rhythm, harmony and lyrics are its elements (Compendium §802.3).
  • Someone else's beat or arrangement. This is exactly what sank Chắc Ai Đó Sẽ Về. Vietnam's culture ministry said the beat (hòa âm) is protected "cũng như giai điệu và ca từ" (just like melody and lyrics) (VnExpress 2014).
    • The GC and Robin Wesley cases show that online "type beats" are owned. Using one without a licence can get a video blocked (Thanh Niên).
  • Samples of actual recordings, even tiny ones. US 6th Circuit: "Get a license or do not sample" (Bridgeport v. Dimension Films, 2005). Re-playing a part yourself avoids the recording right but not the composition right.
  • Visual and set references (MV makers take note). Using an artwork as a reference without permission or credit led to the Come My Way edit (above).

How to clear a sample

  1. Identify two owners:
    • the master (the recording, usually the label);
    • the composition/publishing (the songwriters, usually a publisher).

      You need permission from both (Tracklib, Spotify for Artists, APRA AMCOS).

  2. Ask before release, in writing. Expect a fee and/or a share of royalties, and expect paperwork (Spotify for Artists).
  3. Alternatives:
    • pre-cleared sample services such as Tracklib, which handles the licence and a revenue share (Tracklib);
    • royalty-free sample packs, after checking their licence;
    • re-playing the part (you still need the publishing licence).

Type beats: leasing vs exclusive

  • On BeatStars, non-exclusive licences are leases with limited terms set by each producer. Exclusive deals generally transfer ownership. Read the licence on the cart page before buying (BeatStars Help, BeatStars blog).
  • Practical rules:
    • Keep the receipt and licence.
    • Respect stream/sale caps.
    • Credit the producer.
    • A "free" beat tagged "for non-profit use" is not licensed for a commercial release. The Akasu remake above says exactly that.

Study by remaking, then make it yours

Berklee Online calls copying a song "one of the best ways to hone your production skills" (Berklee Take Note). EDMProd recommends remakes for improving as a producer (EDMProd). MusicRadar warns against copying other artists' creative decisions from reference tracks (MusicRadar).

A workflow built from those sources:

  1. Load the track, match its BPM and key, and mark the sections.
  2. Rebuild it one layer at a time (drums, bass, chords, lead, FX, arrangement) and A/B against the original. This version is study only: don't release it.
  3. Write down the techniques you learned, for example "moombahton dembow pattern + plucked lead" (that is how the VBK tutorial above teaches Hãy Trao Cho Anh).
  4. Start a new project from those techniques with your own melody, chords voicing, drums and sounds. Change the tempo, key and groove too.
  5. Before you release, compare it with the original: does any melody, hook or recognisable section survive? If yes, rewrite it or get a licence.
  6. Credit and license anything you didn't make (samples, leased beats, co-writers).
